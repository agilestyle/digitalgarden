---
title: Who gets to define "responsible"?
date: 2026-01-29
tags: [responsible-ai, power, governance, open-question, politics]
---


The field of responsible AI is not politically neutral. Its institutions, its funders, its most prominent voices — they have interests. And the definitions of "responsible" that win out tend to be the ones that don't threaten those interests.

This is not a conspiracy. It's just how concepts work when they become institutionalised. The word becomes valuable, so it gets contested, and the parties with the most resources usually shape the dominant meaning.

So: who is writing the responsible AI guidelines? Who funds the ethics research? Whose harms get centred in the frameworks, and whose remain unnamed?

I've been struck by how often the AI ethics discourse in the European and American contexts struggles to name capitalism as a relevant variable. The systems causing harm are operating within an economic logic that rewards certain outcomes and punishes others. Responsible AI frameworks that don't engage with that logic might be treating symptoms.

This is still a live question for me. I'm not sure what follows from it practically — but I think naming it is at least a start.
