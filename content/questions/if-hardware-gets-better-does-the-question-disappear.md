---
title: If the hardware gets better, does the question disappear?
tags: [hardware, efficiency, rebound-effect, responsibility, open-question]
---

One of the things I built into AnemoIA was the idea that tracking environmental impact is a first step toward agency — not a solution, just a way of making visible what is usually hidden. But writing the spec I kept bumping into a footnote I couldn't ignore: STT-MRAM memory, already shipping in consumer devices, has demonstrated 97% power reductions over conventional CMOS memory. Endoh and Fukami published it in *Nature* in 2022. That's not a roadmap item. That's now.

So the question I can't settle is: if the hardware solves the efficiency problem, does the ethical question dissolve with it?

My instinct says no — but I want to be honest about why. The obvious counterargument is Jevons. More efficient systems tend to get used more, not less. The rebound effect doesn't care about good intentions. If running a top-tier model costs a fraction of what it does today, the argument for restraint weakens — and with it, perhaps, the habit of asking whether the tool fits the task.

But there's another layer. The environmental cost was always a proxy for something else: the question of whether we're using these systems *thoughtfully*, or just *habitually*. A 97% reduction in energy consumption doesn't change what it means to ask a question well, or to choose the right tool, or to understand what you're delegating and what you're keeping.

Maybe efficiency changes the *urgency* but not the *nature* of the question. Or maybe it removes the one lever that made the question legible to people who wouldn't otherwise ask it at all.

I don't know. But I think it matters whether we're building awareness tools for a transitional moment — or for something more permanent.
