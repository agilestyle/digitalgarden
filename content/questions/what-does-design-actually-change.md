---
title: What does design actually change?
date: 2026-06-03
tags: [design, agency, systems, open-question, practice]
---


I've been in this program for nearly a year now and I'm still not fully settled on what design can actually do in relation to AI systems.

At the optimistic end: design shapes experience, and experience shapes what people accept. A well-designed interface can make a harmful system legible and contestable. A badly designed one hides the system entirely. That's real power.

At the pessimistic end: design is often deployed after the fundamental decisions have already been made. The model is trained, the product is built, the business model is set — and then designers are brought in to make it usable. That's aestheticising rather than shaping.

The question I can't resolve is where along that spectrum most design work actually sits. And whether it's possible to intervene earlier — at the point of conception, at the governance level — or whether that requires a fundamentally different kind of practice.

The programme frames design as a "broad and plural decision-making practice." I believe that. But I also notice how rarely designers are in the rooms where the significant decisions get made.

What changes first: the practice, or the room?
