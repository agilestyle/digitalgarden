---
title: Can transparency be enough?
tags: [transparency, explainability, governance, open-question]
---


The EU AI Act puts a lot of weight on transparency obligations. Disclose that you're using AI. Make decisions explainable. Log outputs. Let people know when they're talking to a machine.

These feel like reasonable starting points. But I keep asking: transparent to whom, and to what end?

Most transparency mechanisms are designed to satisfy regulators, not the people actually affected by algorithmic decisions. The audit trail exists — but it's not legible to someone who just got denied a loan. Explainability often means "we can produce a document explaining this," not "you can understand and contest what happened to you."

There's also a version of transparency that actually legitimises opacity. If an institution follows the prescribed disclosure steps, it becomes harder to challenge the underlying system. The form of accountability substitutes for the substance of it.

I don't have an answer here. But I think the question is: what would transparency look like if it was designed for the person being governed by the system, rather than the institution deploying it?
