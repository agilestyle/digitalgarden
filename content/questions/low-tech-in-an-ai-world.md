---
title: What does low-tech mean now?
tags: [low-tech, media-lab, sustainability, open-question, practice]
---


One of the things I found most unexpected about this program was the Transversal module — the Critical Design/Media Lab — and its commitment to building a "low-tech digital publication."

At first that felt like an aesthetic preference. Simpler. Slower. Less.

But I think there's something more substantive here: low-tech as a political stance. If the dominant tendency in tech is toward more compute, more data, more scale, more automation — then low-tech is a refusal of that as the only direction. It's a way of asking: what can be done with less? What gets preserved when you don't optimise for growth?

What I haven't fully worked out is whether low-tech is a realistic alternative or a critique that works from within a position of privilege. It's easier to choose simplicity when you have access to complexity. Communities that have never had reliable infrastructure experience "low-tech" very differently.

So the question I'm sitting with: is there a version of low-tech that's genuinely emancipatory rather than nostalgic? And what would it look like to design for it?
