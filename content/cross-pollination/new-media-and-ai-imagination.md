Eryk Salvaggio's work sits at an intersection I find genuinely exciting: AI systems, visual culture, and the politics of how these technologies are imagined and represented.

One thing he surfaced that I keep returning to: the images we use to represent AI shape what we think AI is and what we think it can do. Neural networks visualised as glowing orbs and geometric grids. AI "thinking" visualised as a brain scan. These aren't neutral illustrations — they're arguments. They make AI feel more biological, more powerful, more inevitable than it might otherwise seem.

This connects to the [[what-is-intelligence-anyway]] thread in my own notes. The language and imagery of AI is doing political work before any policy is written.

Better Images of AI — the project behind the cover image of the program brochure — is trying to offer alternatives. More mundane, more honest representations. A dataset as a spreadsheet, not a constellation. A model as a statistical process, not a mind. These visual choices matter.

What I'm taking into my practice: the way I communicate about AI systems is itself a design act. Framing matters. Metaphor matters. The imaginary shapes the possible.
