Michelle Thorne from the Green Web Foundation brought something into the program that I hadn't expected to encounter here: a precise, technical account of how the internet's carbon footprint is distributed, and what designers and developers can do about it.

The Green Web Foundation tracks which hosting providers run on renewable energy. That's already a useful tool. But what I took from her session was the broader argument: that the environmental cost of digital systems is a design problem, not just an infrastructure problem. Every decision about where to host, how to architect, what to compute, carries an energy consequence. And those consequences accumulate.

This connects directly to [[the-cost-of-ai]] — but it gave me something more actionable. Not just "AI is expensive" but: here are the points in the stack where choices are made, and here's who makes them.

I've since started thinking about my final project through this lens. What would it mean to design a system that's honest about its resource use? Not just ethically accountable, but materially accountable?

I don't think we talk about this enough in design education. The pixel is never just the pixel.

→ Green Web Foundation: [thegreenwebfoundation.org](https://www.thegreenwebfoundation.org/)
