## Objective

Enhance the collaborative learning experience by helping participants grow and organize thoughts, ideas, and knowledge acquired during the program in creative, open and mindful ways — ultimately developing a digital gardening practice.

## The Three Tasks

### 🌱 Seed

Choose a preferred tool or platform to set up and customise your digital garden. The only requirement is that it can be accessible openly through a public hyperlink.

*Note: you might want to change tools/platforms during the program — that's fine, as long as any new version is updated with the contents from the previous one.*

### 🌿 Grow

Develop a weekly ritual to update, edit and evolve your digital garden in a way that suits you. By definition a digital garden is a personal space — it needs to suit your needs and ways of learning and managing knowledge.

### 🌸 Cross-pollinate

Invite peers to visit your garden, and visit theirs regularly. Think of ways to collaborate and connect gardens. Mutually care about them and the practice. If a garden hasn't been updated in a while, encourage the peer to keep it alive. Help each other grow beautiful gardens.

## Final Assessment (June 2026)

Look back together at the gardens, reflect on the evolution of practice, and make a **5–10 minute presentation** about the digital gardening experience.

**Assessment criteria:**

- **a)** Authenticity and creativity
- **b)** Consistency of practice
- **c)** Collaboration

## Considerations

- A digital garden is not a blog or notepad — though it can include similar things and practices
- By definition it can change, be edited, and evolve organically. It is not about publishing — think "last updated" rather than "published on"
- Enjoy the metaphor: gardening can be hard but also joyful and very satisfying over time
