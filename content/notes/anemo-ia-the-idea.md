---
title: AnemoIA — where the idea came from
date: 2026-04-08
tags: [anemoia, responsible-ai]
---


The project started with a frustration, not a solution.

By the middle of Term II I had spent months reading governance frameworks, ethics principles, and responsible AI guidelines. They all named the same values — transparency, accountability, sustainability, fairness. And they all stopped somewhere before the point where those values touch actual behaviour. The frameworks existed. The practice didn't follow.

I kept coming back to one observation: the tools people actually use to interact with AI are designed to be effortless. That's intentional. Friction is treated as a UX failure. But effortlessness is also a kind of concealment — it hides what the interaction costs, who built the system, what it's optimising for.

The idea behind AnemoIA was simple: what if the interface didn't hide those things?

Not in a punishing way. Not as a guilt mechanism. But as information — present at the right moment, without overwhelming the act of composing a prompt. Every request already has a carbon cost, an energy cost, a water cost. Most chat interfaces simply don't show it. AnemoIA does.

---

The name came later, but it clarified something. *Anemoia* is the feeling of nostalgia for a time you've never lived. I found it fitting because there's a version of AI use that doesn't exist yet — one where people reach for these tools with an awareness of what they're doing, where the defaults aren't the most resource-intensive option, where literacy comes before habit. That version of things feels like somewhere we've almost been, and are still trying to find.

The project is a small, pragmatic step toward it. Not an argument that AI is bad, or that we should use it less. An argument that awareness is the precondition for agency — and that agency is what responsible use actually requires.
