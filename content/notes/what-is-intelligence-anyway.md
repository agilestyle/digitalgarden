---
title: What is intelligence, anyway?
tags: [ai, intelligence, decoding-ai, term-1]
---


One of the first things that happened in [[Decoding AI Systems]] was that someone asked us to define intelligence. Not artificial intelligence — just intelligence. And nobody could.

That moment stuck with me. We spend so much time debating whether AI is "real" intelligence, whether machines can "truly" understand, whether LLMs are just autocomplete or something more — and underneath all of it is a word we haven't agreed on.

The word "artificial" in AI is doing enormous work. It implies there's a natural version we all recognise. But the more I sit with it, the more I think intelligence is a social category, not a biological or computational one. We grant it. We revoke it. We use it to draw lines.

The history of who gets to be considered intelligent is not a comfortable history.

So when a company says their model is "intelligent," I now hear a claim being made. A political one. And that reframe changed how I look at the whole field.
