---
title: Responsible for whom?
tags: [responsible-ai, power, ethics, governance, term-2]
---


The more I use the phrase "responsible AI," the less stable it feels.

Responsible to whom? On whose terms? Measured by what? The word does a lot of ideological work. It implies a shared understanding of responsibility that doesn't actually exist — it collapses very different ideas about what harm is, who counts as affected, and what obligations follow from causing harm.

In the European context, which we spent a lot of time on in Term II, "responsible AI" tends to be defined through a legal-risk framework: what could make you liable? That's a start, but it optimises for the concerns of organisations — not people. The EU AI Act is largely a compliance document. Compliance and ethics aren't the same thing.

Nushin Yazdani at Superrr Lab works on what a feminist approach to AI governance looks like — one that starts from the needs of marginalised communities rather than the risk appetite of institutions. That inversion matters. It changes who is protected and from what.

I don't think "responsible AI" is a bad goal. I think it's an underspecified one, and that underspecification is doing political work. Whoever fills it in wins.
